Mixed Precision Lab — FP16 without the tears

FP16 numbers are half the size of FP32 → roughly 2–8× faster matmuls on tensor cores and half the memory (hello, batch size 512). The price: FP16 can only represent a narrow range of values, and small gradients silently become 0. Three mini-labs below.

💡 5 exponent bits ⇒ FP16 maxes out at 65 504 (overflow → inf) and normal numbers bottom out at 6.1×10⁻⁵ (subnormals crawl to 6×10⁻⁸, with terrible precision). Typical DDPM gradients live around 10⁻⁴…10⁻⁸ — squarely on the cliff edge. Click the tiny gradient preset and watch FP16 read 0.0.

💡 The whole trick is one multiplication. Because backprop is linear, scaling the loss by 2¹⁶ scales every gradient by 2¹⁶ — lifting them out of the underflow zone before they are ever stored in FP16. The optimizer must see honest gradients, so scaler.step() divides the scale back out in FP32, where tiny values are perfectly representable. Push the scale to 2²⁴ with a large-ish gradient: now you overflow to inf instead — which is why the scale is tuned dynamically (tab 3).

💡 Read the callback as a story: autocast makes the forward pass cheap; GradScaler makes the backward pass safe; and the master weights + optimizer state stay in FP32 the whole time — that’s the “mixed” in mixed precision.
