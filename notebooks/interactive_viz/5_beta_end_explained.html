beta_end — one scalar, an entire noise schedule

DDPMScheduler(beta_end=0.01) looks like a minor argument. It isn’t: this single number pivots the whole β line, which reshapes every ᾱt, which redefines what “noise level at timestep t” means — the very thing the model memorized during training. Four tabs: turn the knob and watch the whole curve move; trace the number’s journey from training notebook 19 into this one (and why it must be re-declared); break the match and watch reconstructions shatter; and the reason 0.01 beat the paper’s default 0.02. Reading the chart
With 0.02, ᾱ is
  effectively zero long before t = 999 — the shaded tail is timesteps whose images are indistinguishable
  from pure noise, so training examples drawn there teach the model almost nothing. With 0.01, signal
  survives deeper into the schedule and the destruction rate is spread more evenly — the property the cosine
  schedule was designed for. Toggle to the rate view: the taller and earlier a curve’s hump, the more
  abruptly it burns information.




🔑 Key takeaway: beta_end is the noise schedule’s DNA. It was
chosen (0.01) and baked into the model’s weights in notebook 19; notebook 20 must re-declare it in every
scheduler because the checkpoint stores weights, not the schedule. Match it and the model is at home;
change it and every timestep lies to the model.
