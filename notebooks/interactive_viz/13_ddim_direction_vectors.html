The update has three components — signal, direction, fresh noise — and the middle one has the strangest name. Why is a noise prediction called a “direction pointing to xt”? Project image-space onto the two directions that matter for this trajectory: the clean image x₀ (horizontal) and its noise ε (vertical). Every state is xs = √ᾱs·x₀ + √(1−ᾱs)·ε, i.e. the point ( √ᾱs, √(1−ᾱs) ) — and since ᾱs + (1−ᾱs) = 1, every such point sits exactly on the unit circle. The whole diffusion trajectory is the arc from (0,1) = pure noise to (1,0) = clean.

Read it like an accountant
The schedule demands that xt−1 carry exactly 1 − ᾱt−1 of noise variance in total. The update pays that bill from two accounts: the predicted noise (deterministic, reuses εθ) and the fresh noise (random). η slides money between the accounts; the bill never changes. Subtracting inside the square root is what leaves room for z.

Key takeaway
Each DDIM step is an interpolation: move toward the predicted clean image (√ᾱt−1·x̂₀), stay on the established trajectory (the direction term — the arc of tab ②), and optionally explore (σ·z). The direction term is why DDIM’s denoising is a structured walk along a known road rather than DDPM’s random stagger — and why the road can be traveled in big hops.
