Notebook 19 wrote the reverse step by hand; Notebook 20 calls sched.step(noise, t, x_t). The professor’s claim: they are the same two lines. Four tabs: open the box and watch the inputs meet those two lines, walk each line with its math and live values, inspect the res dataclass drawer by drawer, and diff the two sampling loops — where one N20 line lights up seven N19 lines at once. ⚖️ One honest footnote: diffusers’ DDPMScheduler defaults its noise variance to the posterior β̃t (variance_type="fixed_small"); Notebook 19 uses σt = √βt (the "fixed_large" option). Both are standard DDPM choices, within ~1% over most of the schedule — the deterministic part of the step, verified above, is identical to machine precision.
