click any pixel of x_t to trace its value: x_t[i,j] = √ᾱ·x₀[i,j] + √(1−ᾱ)·ε[i,j]
Why can we jump straight to any t? Adding Gaussian noise to Gaussian noise gives… Gaussian noise, with variances that simply add. So 1000 tiny noise additions collapse into one big, exactly-equivalent addition — the closed form above. This is a huge deal for training: for each image in a batch, before_batch draws a random t (0–999), computes x_t in one line, and asks the model to predict the ε it just used. No simulation loop, no stored trajectory — every batch trains the model at hundreds of noise levels simultaneously. (The reverse process gets no such shortcut: generation really does loop 1000 times.)
