Triangular timestep sampling: train where it matters

During training, every image gets a random timestep t before being noised. Uniform sampling spends equal effort on trivially-easy (t≈0) and hopelessly-hard (t≈999) cases. The notebook’s noisify() instead draws t from a triangular distribution peaking mid-schedule — exactly where the denoising signal is richest. Draw samples and watch the two histograms fill; the dashed lines are the exact expected shapes, and both charts share one y-axis so heights are directly comparable.

Why the middle matters — the three regimes

    

      
t ≈ 0 — too easy. Input is nearly clean; predicting the tiny noise is trivial and the gradient teaches almost nothing.

      
t ≈ 500 — the sweet spot. ⭐ Signal and noise are balanced: the model must truly understand garment structure to separate them. Richest gradients live here.

      
t ≈ 999 — near-impossible. Input is ~pure noise; the target is essentially unguessable, so the loss is dominated by irreducible randomness.
