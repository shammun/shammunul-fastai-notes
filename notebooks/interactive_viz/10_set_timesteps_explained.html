set_timesteps(200) — the dial that never touches training

The question this page answers: “does set_timesteps(200) mean the noising / forward process now uses 200 steps?” — No. Training used all 1000 steps and is finished; the dial only chooses which timesteps the sampler visits. Four tabs: the training/sampling wall (fiddle one side, watch the other never move), what the call actually builds, why jumping between non-adjacent steps is mathematically legal — proven numerically — and a quiz on the doc’s three misconceptions.

…and the honest caveat
With a perfect
  ε the proof on the left is exact: one jump of Δ lands on the identical point as Δ chained
  single steps — the formula truly doesn’t care about adjacency. With a real model, each visited
  step re-evaluates εθ and slightly corrects the aim; jumping means fewer corrections.
  That — and only that — is why FID creeps from 3.7 (1000 visits) to 4.5 (200 visits): the jumps are legal,
  the corrections are just rarer.
