Diffusion in 3D — watch a whole dataset melt into a Gaussian

Images are just points in a (very) high-dimensional space, so here is diffusion on a space we can actually see: a 2-D dataset (the vertical disc) pushed through time (the horizontal axis). Every dot follows x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, scrub time, and flip the direction to “play it as sampling”.

💡 What this shape is telling you: at t=0 the point cloud has structure (moons, arms, blobs) — that structure is the data distribution. As ᾱ_t shrinks, every point slides along a straight line toward its own noise target ε, and by t≈999 the slice is a featureless Gaussian ball — the same simple distribution regardless of what data you started from. That is the entire reason sampling can begin with torch.randn: the destination of the forward process is known in closed form. The reverse direction (flip the toggle!) is what the trained model learns to walk. Fashion-MNIST works identically, just in 1×32×32 = 1024 dimensions instead of 2.
