.prev_sample — what actually travels around the loop

The companion deep-dive page opens sched.step(); this page keeps it closed and follows the plumbing: what flows between the stations of the sampling loop, what type each packet is (two of them are dataclasses, not tensors!), why the field is called prev_sample when it moves you forward, and what breaks the moment you forget the extraction.


    
What’s happening

  




How to read it: one lap = one iteration. The return wire is the star: it carries
.prev_sample — a plain tensor, xt−1 — back into the x_t box, and the big
counter drops by one. Run the laps and watch the box’s image clean up as t falls.

“Previous” in the countdown = forward in the denoising



  
  

    t700
    
    
  




The trick of the name: timesteps count down (999 → 0), so the
“previous timestep” t−1 is the next place the walker stands — one step closer to the
clean image. prev_sample is named after the timestep index, not the direction of travel.


🧭 Every call at timestep t hands you xt−1: current = xt,
“previous” = xt−1 — previous in the timestep sequence, which is forward in
the denoising process. The loop is just this handoff, 1000 times.








Why hand back a dataclass instead of a bare tensor?



  
🧩 Flexibility Different schedulers return different extras — DDIM’s output has the same fields, so DDPMScheduler → DDIMScheduler is a one-line swap and the loop never changes.

  
🔍 Debugging & previews .pred_original_sample lets you peek at what the model currently believes the finished image is — at any step, for free.

  
📦 Extensibility New fields can be added later without breaking a single existing loop — impossible with bare tensors or positional tuples.

  
📝 Clarity result.prev_sample reads like documentation; result[0] reads like a puzzle.





  
Accessing the optional field

  
result = sched.step(noise, t, x_t)
x_t_minus_1     = result.prev_sample           # what the loop iterates on
predicted_clean = result.pred_original_sample  # for visualization; may be None





  
Summary

  

    
QUESTIONANSWER

    
What does sched.step() return?A SchedulerOutput dataclass with multiple fields

    
What is .prev_sample?The denoised image at timestep t−1 (xt−1)

    
Why “prev”?Previous timestep index — which is forward in the denoising countdown

    
What happens without it?x_t becomes a SchedulerOutput object; the next model(x_t, t) call raises a TypeError (tab ② demonstrates it)

    
What’s inside sched.step()?Exactly two lines of math — the companion page sched.step, the black box opened takes them apart
