The Variance Schedule: β, α and the All-Important ᾱ

Four cheap tensors, computed once in DDPMCB.__init__, drive everything in DDPM. β_t is how much noise step t adds; α_t = 1−β_t is how much signal it keeps; ᾱ_t = ∏α is the signal surviving all steps up to t. Hover the chart to read exact values, click the curve chips to toggle them, and drag the sliders to see how the schedule reshapes.

β_min 0.0001 β_max 0.020 n_steps 1000
# exactly the lines from DDPMCB.__init__ in the notebook: self.β = torch.linspace(β_min, β_max, n_steps) # tiny per-step noise variances self.α = 1. - self.β # per-step signal keep-rate self.ᾱ = torch.cumprod(self.α, dim=0) # TOTAL signal kept after t steps self.σ = self.β.sqrt() # noise std used during sampling
Three things to notice. ① Each individual β_t is tiny (max 0.02 = keep 98% per step) — but 1000 compounding steps drive ᾱ_t to ≈0, which is why the final image is pure noise. ② The pair √ᾱ_t and √(1−ᾱ_t) are the exact coefficients in the closed-form jump x_t = √ᾱ·x₀ + √(1−ᾱ)·ε — hover any t to read them off. ③ Try raising β_max: destruction finishes early and many late timesteps are "wasted" on pure noise; lower it too far and x_T never becomes fully Gaussian, so sampling starts from the wrong distribution. The defaults (0.0001 → 0.02, T=1000) are the DDPM paper's carefully tuned compromise.
