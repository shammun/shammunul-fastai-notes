SGD in 3D: learning rate × batch size on a loss surface

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. A gently wavy loss landscape with its contour map projected below, the way optimization figures are usually drawn. The orange ball is the model's two weights; Reset ball drops it somewhere new, and the dotted trail records its full descent path.

Try: lr = 0.40 — the ball overshoots across the steep direction and diverges (the steepest curvature sets the speed limit, here lr ≈ 0.3). lr = 0.02 — it crawls and settles short of the bottom. batch size = 1 — a drunken stagger that still finds the valley on average; batch size = 256 — a smooth, confident descent (gradient noise shrinks like 1/√bs). One honest subtlety: with a constant learning rate, SGD never truly stops — noise keeps it hovering in a small “noise ball” around the minimum. Real training therefore decays the learning rate, and this demo does the same: once the ball enters the basin, the effective lr is annealed toward 0 (watch the readout), so it settles and reports CONVERGED ✓. Use the speed slider to slow the dance down or fast-forward it.
